Global satellite orbit dataset

01
Dataset overview

This dataset tracks active satellites currently in Earth orbit, compiled by the Union of Concerned Scientists. Each row is one satellite — who operates it, what it's used for, and the orbital mechanics that keep it up there.

I picked the UCS Satellite Database because it gives a real, current snapshot of every operational satellite in orbit — not a historical or simulated dataset. It sits at the intersection of my interest in aerospace and the kind of structured, numeric data that's genuinely useful for statistical analysis. With over 7,000 satellites tracked, it captures the full diversity of the industry: national space agencies, private companies, military programs, and university projects all in one table, each with hard orbital mechanics numbers (mass, altitude, period) alongside the human/organizational side (who owns it, what it's for). That mix of categorical and numerical detail is exactly why it works well for this activity.

03
Variable identification
Categorical
  • Country of Operator — nominal, no inherent order
  • Purpose — nominal, describes mission type
  • Users — nominal, ownership sector
Numerical
  • Perigee (km) — continuous, ratio scale
  • Apogee (km) — continuous, ratio scale
  • Inclination (°) — continuous, interval/ratio
  • Launch mass (kg) — continuous, ratio scale
Categorical: Country of Operator, Purpose, and Users are categorical because their values are labels, not quantities — "Purpose" being "Communications" or "Earth Observation" can't be measured or ranked numerically. All three are nominal scale, since there's no natural order between categories (e.g. "Military" isn't inherently higher or lower than "Commercial").

Numerical: Perigee, Apogee, Inclination, and Launch Mass are numerical because they represent measurable quantities from real instruments. All four are ratio scale — they have a true zero (0 km altitude or 0 kg mass genuinely means none), and ratios between values are meaningful (twice the mass really is twice as heavy). All four are also continuous, since they can take any value within a range (e.g. 51.6° or 51.63°), not just whole-number counts.
